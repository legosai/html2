Early Life & Determination about Marie Curie

Born in Warsaw, Poland, Marie grew up during a time when women were barred from attending university in her home country. Refusing to let that stop her education, she attended the secret Flying University, an underground network of clandestine educational classes. In 1891, after saving money working as a governess, she moved to Paris to study physics and mathematics at the Sorbonne, often living on very little food and facing harsh winter cold to earn her degrees.

Breakthrough Discoveries

Discovered New Elements (1898): Discovered Polonium (named for Poland) and Radium. Coined "Radioactivity": Created the term to radiation emitted by elements. First Nobel Prize (1903): Awarded in Physics; became the first woman to win a Nobel Prize. Second Nobel Prize (1911): Awarded in Chemistry; became the first person to win two Nobel

Impact & Legacy

  1. WWI Support: Created mobile X-ray units ("Little Curies") to treat over one million soldiers.
  2. Cancer Research: Pioneered the foundation for modern nuclear physics and targeted radiation therapy.
  3. Academic Trailblazer: Became the first female professor at the Sorbonne.
